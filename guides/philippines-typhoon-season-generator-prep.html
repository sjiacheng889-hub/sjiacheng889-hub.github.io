Typhoon Season in the Philippines: Backup Generator Prep Checklist

By Tommy, Export Sales Manager at Zhiqiang Power · Tommy@zhiqiang-power.com · Last reviewed 2026-09-17 against factory load-bank data

Every year the same pattern repeats: the first major typhoon takes out feeders, and the buildings that lose power longest are the ones whose standby generator had not been started under load since the last season. This checklist is what we tell our Philippine customers to work through in May and June, before the weather decides for them.

It applies to standby sets of any brand. Where a step needs numbers, we use our own catalogue data, the fuel curves and maintenance intervals published on each model page.

The short answer

Typhoon season runs roughly June to November, with the sharpest demand for standby power from July to October. A set that is load-tested, fueled and battery-checked before June keeps running when the grid does not.

  • ›Test under real load, not at idle: a monthly 30-minute run on the load bank or a transfer test burns off the carbon that light-load running leaves behind.
  • ›Fuel: top up before the season, budget roughly 24 L/h per 100 kW at full load (see the fuel guide for your exact model), and plan refill access while roads are still passable.
  • ›Batteries and coolant are the two most common no-start causes after a storm; check terminals and specific gravity monthly during the season.
  • ›AMF (auto mains failure) panels start the set without anyone on site. For unattended buildings, retrofitting one before the season beats paying a guard to run it manually.
  • ›The Philippines is a 60Hz market: spares, chargers and any replacement set must be 60Hz-calibrated. Order consumables in May, not in the first week of a storm.

May-June: the pre-season service window

Work through the 250-hour service items whether the hours are due or not: oil and filters, fuel filters and water separator, coolant level and hoses, belt tension, and the battery. A set that sits idle accumulates condensation in the tank and sulfation on the terminals, and both show up exactly once, on the worst night of the year.

If the set has not carried a real load in the past twelve months, book a load-bank test. Idle runs prove almost nothing; a 100% and 110% check proves the set will take the building. This is the same test every unit of ours passes before shipment, and the record ships with the set.

Fuel math for a multi-day outage

Size the tank against the outage you plan for, not the one you hope for. A 100 kW set at three-quarters load burns about 18.5 L/h, so a 24-hour outage needs roughly 450 liters plus reserve. The fuel guide shows how to compute this for any model in the catalogue, and the product pages print the full load curve.

Polish or replace fuel that has sat more than six to twelve months, and keep the tank as full as practical during the season: the air gap above the diesel is where condensation forms.

Spares and the 60Hz detail

Hold the consumables on site before June: the filters for your engine model, belts, fuses, coolant and at least two sets of batteries. Freight during a typhoon week is either slow or impossible, and our parts dispatch of 1-5 working days assumes roads and ports are open.

Everything electrical must suit 60Hz: battery chargers, timers and any replacement equipment. If a second set is on the shopping list for 2026, order it 60Hz-calibrated and load-tested at 1800 rpm, which is how every set we ship to the Philippines leaves the factory.

During the season: the monthly ten-minute check

Once a month, run the set under load for at least 30 minutes, log the volts, frequency, oil pressure and coolant temperature, and walk the exhaust run while it is hot. After any grid outage, refill the tank the same week and check the battery specific gravity. Ten minutes a month is the whole program.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is typhoon season in the Philippines?▼

Roughly June through November, with the highest frequency of storms from July to October, though strong systems can arrive outside those months. Standby generator preparation should be finished by June, and the monthly loaded run should continue through November.

How often should a standby generator be load tested?▼

Run it under load at least monthly for 30 minutes, and arrange a full load-bank test (100% and 110%) at least once a year, ideally before typhoon season. Idle running alone leaves carbon in the exhaust and glazes the bores, and proves nothing about the set taking the building load.

Do I need an AMF panel for typhoon outages?▼

For any building that must recover power without a person on site, yes. An AMF panel detects the mains failure, starts the set and transfers the load automatically, usually within tens of seconds. For attended sites a manual changeover can work, but only if someone trained is present at 2 a.m.
