kW to kVA: How to Size a Diesel Generator Without Guessing

By Tommy, Export Sales Manager at Zhiqiang Power · Tommy@zhiqiang-power.com · Last reviewed 2026-09-17 against factory load-bank data

Most sizing mistakes we see are unit mistakes, not engineering mistakes: a buyer totals the kW on his equipment list, orders "a 100 kVA set" for a 100 kW load, and the generator arrives about 20% too small. This page gives you the conversion, the margin rules and the motor-start check in that order.

The short answer

For three-phase industrial generators the rated power factor is 0.8, so kVA = kW ÷ 0.8. A 100 kW requirement means a 125 kVA set, before you add any margin.

  • ›kW is the real power your equipment does work with; kVA is the apparent power the generator must supply. At cos φ 0.8, 1 kVA delivers 0.8 kW.
  • ›Add 20–25% reserve on top of your running load so the set sits in its efficient band and can absorb peaks without tripping.
  • ›Direct-on-line motor starts pull 3–6× full-load current for a few seconds; the largest motor often decides the set size, not the total load.
  • ›kW vs kVA also matters when comparing quotes: some suppliers lead with the bigger number. Always ask which rating the figure refers to.

Motor starting current: the number that really sizes the set

A motor started direct-on-line draws three to six times its rated current until it reaches speed. If your largest machine is a 30 kW pump on DOL starting, the generator has to swallow that inrush without a voltage dip deep enough to trip contactors. In practice this means checking the alternator datasheet for motor-starting capability, sometimes choosing a frame larger than the running kW suggests, or using star-delta or soft starting on the biggest motors.

How much margin should you add?

For standby duty, size against the essential load you will actually shed to, plus 20–25%. For prime or continuous duty, aim to sit between 50% and 80% of prime power during normal running: below that band the engine glazes, above it you are living on the overload allowance.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is 100 kW the same as 100 kVA?▼

No. At the standard 0.8 power factor, 100 kW needs a 125 kVA generator, and a 100 kVA set can only deliver 80 kW. If two suppliers quote different-looking numbers for the same machine, one is probably quoting kVA and the other kW.

What power factor do diesel generators use?▼

Industrial three-phase gensets are rated at 0.8 lagging, per the alternator nameplate and ISO 8528-1 practice. Single-phase loads should be balanced across the three phases; a badly unbalanced load derates the set and heats the alternator.

Should I buy a bigger generator “just in case”?▼

One size of headroom is sensible; two is not. A set that never passes 30% load runs wet, glazes its bores and needs early top-end maintenance. Give us the load list and growth plan and we will size for where you will actually be in two years.
