Importing Gensets from China: Three Mistakes We See Every Season

By Tommy, Export Sales Manager at Zhiqiang Power · Tommy@zhiqiang-power.com · Last reviewed 2026-09-17 against factory load-bank data

We handle export packing, documentation and shipment from Nansha and Qinzhou for our five ASEAN markets, so the mistakes below are the ones that actually cross our desk, collected from real consignments rather than theory. None of them is hard to avoid once you know they exist.

The short answer

The three failures we see most on ASEAN generator shipments are the 50Hz/60Hz mix-up for the Philippines, Form E or HS-code errors that cost the duty preference or stall clearance, and ordering to open-skid lead times when a silent or containerized unit is needed.

  • ›Frequency: the Philippines runs 60Hz while Vietnam, Thailand, Malaysia and Myanmar run 50Hz. A wrong-frequency set is a return shipment, not a setting.
  • ›Documents: ACFTA Form E can qualify the consignment for preferential duty, but only when the HS code (gensets classify under 8502), consignor and invoice all agree.
  • ›Lead time: open-skid models dispatch in 15-25 days; silent canopy and containerized builds take 30-40 days. Ordering late in the season means paying for speed or waiting out the project.
  • ›Every set ships with the factory load-bank test record (100% and 110% checks) so the numbers on the nameplate are the numbers you measured at acceptance.

Mistake 1: shipping a 50Hz set to a 60Hz grid

The Philippines is the only 60Hz market in our region, and it catches buyers who standardize purchases across countries. A 50Hz set on a 60Hz grid turns every motor about 17% slow and runs magnetic equipment hot; there is no field switch that fixes it. The conversion means governor and fuel-pump recalibration, AVR re-setting and a new nameplate rating, done properly at the factory.

The fix costs nothing: state the destination grid in the enquiry. Every set in this catalogue is built to order with dual-frequency calibration, and the product page prints the supported voltage and frequency combinations.

Mistake 2: losing the duty preference to a document mismatch

Under ACFTA, a valid Form E can qualify the consignment for preferential import duty, subject to the HS code, origin rules and the destination tariff schedule. What breaks it in practice is small: the HS code on the certificate not matching the invoice description (compression-ignition gensets classify under heading 8502), consignor details differing between documents, or the certificate issued after the vessel sailed when the rules require otherwise.

We prepare the full pack, commercial invoice, packing list, Form E and the load-bank test record, and we cross-check the three numbers that must agree: HS code, consignor and quantities. Ask your broker to confirm the destination tariff line before the documents are issued, not after the ship arrives.

Mistake 3: planning to the wrong lead time

Open-skid builds dispatch in 15-25 days. A silent canopy adds fabrication and acoustic testing, and a containerized set adds CSC container work: plan 30-40 days for those. The trap is seasonal, Philippine buyers ordering before typhoon season and island projects before the dry-season logistics window often discover the canopy lead time when the calendar has already closed on it.

The fix is to fix the configuration early. Canopy type, frequency, tank size and controller are the long-lead decisions; once they are frozen, the build slot is easy to hold. If your deadline is tight, say so in the enquiry and we will tell you honestly which options still fit.

Frequently Asked Questions

What documents come with a generator shipped from China?▼

Commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, ACFTA Form E for preferential duty where applicable, fumigation certificate for the wooden packing, and the factory load-bank test record for your specific unit. We cross-check the HS code and consignor details against the invoice before the documents are issued.

What is the lead time for a silent type generator from China?▼

Plan 30-40 days for silent canopy or containerized builds, and 15-25 days for open-skid sets, before ocean transit. Typhoon-season and dry-season deadlines should be planned against the longer figure, because the canopy and container work is what takes the extra time.

Which HS code do diesel generator sets use?▼

Compression-ignition generator sets classify under heading 8502, with the exact subheading depending on the power rating. The HS code on the Form E, the invoice and the destination declaration must all agree, or the preferential duty can be refused at clearance. Confirm the tariff line with your broker for your country.
